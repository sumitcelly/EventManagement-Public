Yes, **money can be made by developing an event ticketing platform today**, but it's important to recognize that the market is **highly competitive**, and success depends on **differentiation, execution, and targeting the right niche**.

Here’s a breakdown of the opportunity:

---

### ✅ **Why It’s Still Viable**

1. **Demand for Events is Strong**
   Post-pandemic, live events (concerts, sports, conferences, festivals, etc.) are booming again.

2. **High Fees from Big Players**
   Platforms like **Ticketmaster**, **Eventbrite**, and **StubHub** charge **high service fees**, which frustrates both organizers and attendees. This leaves room for lower-cost or better-value alternatives.

3. **Niche Markets are Underserved**
   Local events, community centers, independent venues, and smaller festivals often find existing platforms too expensive or limited in features.

4. **New Tech = New Opportunities**
   Emerging technologies like **blockchain**, **AI**, and **dynamic pricing** can offer a unique angle (e.g., anti-scalping measures, fraud prevention, personalized offers).

---

### ⚠️ **Challenges to Overcome**

* **Strong Competition**: Big players dominate the market and have brand recognition and infrastructure.
* **User Trust & Security**: Ticketing involves payment and identity verification. You must build a secure and trustworthy platform.
* **Marketing**: Getting event organizers to switch platforms is hard unless you offer a **clear advantage**.
* **Logistics**: Managing refunds, cancellations, and customer service is operationally intense.

---

### 💡 **How to Stand Out (Ideas)**

* **Low-fee or zero-fee model** with revenue from upsells (e.g., merch, VIP access).
* **All-in-one tool for small organizers** with CRM, marketing, and ticketing in one.
* **White-label solution** for organizations to brand as their own.
* **Web3 ticketing** for transparency and resale control (NFTs or blockchain-based).
* **Hyperlocal focus** (e.g., only high school/college events, local sports, indie theaters).

---

### 💰 Monetization Strategies

* Ticketing fees (flat rate or % of sales)
* Premium features for organizers (analytics, marketing tools)
* Ads and sponsorships
* Upselling (food, merch, parking, VIP)
* Data insights (with proper consent)

---

### 🔚 Bottom Line

Yes, **you can make money**, but **only if you solve a real pain point** in a specific market. Don’t try to be the next Ticketmaster—**start small, solve a niche problem better**, and grow from there.
